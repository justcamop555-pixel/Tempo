## Tempo 1.0.294

**New: camera-relative movement — with calibration, a hotkey, and a Live Debug view that shows exactly what it's doing**

### Camera-relative movement (advanced)

Hold a direction and Tempo keeps you travelling that way as you swing the camera,
re-mixing W/A/S/D in real time. Circle-strafe an enemy, or keep running one way while
you look another.

**What it is honestly doing.** Tempo cannot *read* the game's camera — that lives
inside another process. It **estimates** the camera by integrating your raw mouse
movement, and steers against that estimate. Two things follow, and they are inherent:

- The estimate **drifts** when the camera turns without mouse input (a cutscene, a
  vehicle, aim-snap). There's a hotkey to re-centre it.
- In-game **mouse acceleration** breaks the straight-line mouse→degrees relationship
  the estimate depends on. Turn it off.

Also worth knowing: most third-person games are *already* camera-relative when you hold
W, so the mode that does real work is **world-locked** — the one that keeps your heading
as the camera moves.

Everything is tunable in Settings → *Camera-relative movement*: mode, sensitivity,
smoothing, anti-jitter, update rate, stick deadzone, pad look speed. Keyboard, mouse
and gamepad are all supported.

### Calibration that actually works

The measurement has to happen **inside the game** — and you can't click a "Stop" button
to finish, because moving the mouse over to the button would add hundreds of counts of
movement to the very number being measured.

So it's gated on a **held key** instead: hold **F10**, turn your camera exactly one full
circle, release. The keyboard adds no mouse movement, the measurement stays clean, and
you never have to leave the game.

### It only touches the game you armed it in

Suppressing W/A/S/D is *global*. Arming this and then alt-tabbing to Discord would
otherwise have silently eaten every "w", "a", "s" and "d" you typed — a nasty trap.

Tempo now captures whichever window was in front **when you armed it** (which is the
game, because you arm it with a hotkey while playing) and leaves your keys completely
alone everywhere else. Interception resumes by itself when you switch back.

Emergency stop always disarms it and releases every held key.

### Anti-jitter, verified

A keyboard can only express 8 directions, so a heading resting exactly on the boundary
between two of them would rattle the keys many times a second. A hysteresis band plus a
minimum dwell time fixes it: **2,000 samples dithering on a boundary now produce zero
key changes**, while a genuine turn still commits promptly.

### Live Debug got much stronger

- **The movement system is now visible**: the armed window, whether it's currently
  acting, the estimated camera angle, the mouse rate — and the line that matters:
  `you press W  →  Tempo sends W+A`. If the character goes the wrong way, you can now
  see *why*.
- **Movement trace** toggle: a line on every key change plus a 1 Hz heartbeat.
- **Always on top**: the debug window used to sit *behind* the game you were trying to
  debug, which made it useless in exactly the case it was most needed.
- **The stats header is selectable and colour-coded** — warnings amber, failures red,
  armed states green — instead of a wall of identical grey text you couldn't even copy
  a line out of.

Windows may show "Unknown publisher" because the app isn't code-signed — click More info → Run anyway. It's safe.
