## Tempo 1.0.262

**Speaker labels gain sight: AI face & mouth analysis (experimental)**

Until now the "Speaker 1/2" labels could only *hear* — voice pitch and pauses. This
release adds *sight*:

**Settings → Live Captions → "AI face & mouth analysis (experimental)"**

When enabled, Tempo watches the video in the foreground window a few times a second:

1. **Windows' built-in on-device face detector** (the same AI the Photos app uses) finds
   the faces — nothing to download, nothing leaves your PC, nothing is recorded.
2. Each face is tracked across frames so it keeps a stable number.
3. The **mouth region** of every face is compared frame-to-frame — a talking mouth makes
   steady motion there.
4. While speech is heard, the face whose mouth is clearly moving becomes the active
   speaker. Sight beats the voice-only guess whenever both are available; the moment no
   face is visible, labels fall back to voice matching exactly as before.

Works with both caption sources (Windows Live Captions and Tempo's own engine).

### Verified in testing
- A face on screen is detected and tracked through the whole pipeline.
- Motion **outside** the mouth region is correctly ignored (waving hands, body movement).
- Mouth movement flips the verdict to that face.
- Face slots recycle across scene cuts — a real bug caught during testing: the tracker
  used to go blind after the first few faces it ever saw.

### Honest limits (also in the tooltip)
- The speaker's face must be visible in the foreground window.
- Small faces carry little mouth signal; scene cuts re-shuffle numbers.
- It costs some CPU, which is why it ships **off by default** — one checkbox turns it on.
- It's a hint that improves labels — never identification.

Technical note: Tempo now targets the Windows-SDK flavour of .NET 8 to reach the OS face
AI, which grows the single-file exe by ~15 MB. Still fully offline, still one file.

Windows may show "Unknown publisher" because the app isn't code-signed — click More info → Run anyway. It's safe.
