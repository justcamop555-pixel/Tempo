## Tempo 1.0.309

**A new Second Cursor you can drive with a second mouse — and an honest word about what it can and can't do.**

This one is experimental. It works well for what it was built for, but Windows puts a hard ceiling on it, and I'd rather tell you the limits up front than pretend they aren't there.

### What it is
A second, real-looking Windows 11 mouse pointer that Tempo draws and controls. You can park it anywhere across both monitors and spam-click there — so you can **AFK-grind a windowed game on monitor 2 while your real mouse stays free on monitor 1**. It's off by default; turn it on with **"Show a second mouse cursor"** on the Clicker tab.

- **Right-click the cursor** for its menu: grab & place, spam-click here, colour, size, shape, spam speed.
- **Grab & place** drops it wherever you click — and it ignores that click so nothing underneath is triggered by accident.
- **Emergency-Stop** stops it, like everything else in Tempo.

### Drive it with a SECOND real mouse
Plug in a second mouse and tick **"Let a 2nd real mouse control it."** Then:

- **Pick it from the "Which mouse:" list** — shown by real product name (e.g. *Razer DeathAdder Essential*). It auto-detects a mouse the moment you plug one in, remembers your choice, and re-binds automatically after a reconnect.
- **Left / right click**, **middle-click** = start/stop spam, **scroll wheel**, and **press-and-hold / drag** all work at the second cursor.
- **Your real pointer stays pinned in place** while you move the second mouse, so the main cursor doesn't wander.
- **Live debug shows both mice** — a per-mouse readout proves Tempo is reading each one independently (▶ marks the one driving the second cursor), with live move/click counts and a "real cursor: HELD STILL / free" line.

### It is NOT perfect — here's the honest part
Windows has exactly **one** hardware cursor. A truly independent second pointer from a second mouse is a kernel-driver feature, and no safe user-space app (Tempo included) can do it. So:

- **Best on windowed / visible-cursor apps and games** — RPGs, idle/clicker, strategy, browser games, and the Windows desktop.
- **Cursor-locked mouse-look games (most FPS) can't be split.** A game that captures the cursor for aiming owns it; a second cursor can't click at a separate spot there.
- **Raw-input games read your MAIN mouse too**, so the two mice aren't perfectly isolated while such a game is focused.
- **Clicking "at the second cursor" briefly borrows the real pointer** for the instant of the click, then hands it back.

If your use is the AFK / second-monitor / windowed-game case it was built for, it does the job. Anywhere else, treat it as experimental and tell me what breaks.
